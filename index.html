A data story about Australia · 2022–2025

Live Well, Australia

How lifestyle shapes our health — state by state. Smoking, diet, drinking, activity and weight vary a surprising amount across the country. Do we live longer where we live better?

10.6% of adults smoke daily
23.9% meet the activity guidelines
1 in 4 exceed the alcohol guideline
Part 1

The geography of lifestyle

Lifestyle is not spread evenly across the map. The same behaviour can be twice as common in one state as in another. Four different map idioms — a filled choropleth, a proportional-symbol map, a dot-density map and a hand-built tile grid — show where Australians are heaviest, where they smoke most, where they drink most, and where diets fall short.

Part 2

Who lives well?

Zooming in on people rather than places. A report-card heatmap compares every state on every factor at once; a dumbbell lays bare the gap between men and women; a socioeconomic gradient shows activity rising with wealth; and the trend line charts smoking's long decline.

Part 3

Lifestyle & wellbeing — the payoff

Do lifestyle choices and health outcomes move together? The connected scatter traces the link between diet and weight across states; the small multiples give the whole picture factor by factor; and the scorecard ranks the nation's biggest lifestyle gaps.

Part 4

How Australia plays

Only a quarter of adults meet the full activity-and-strength guideline — yet 86% move in some way. The national AusPlay survey shows what Australians do, how often, and where they are most active.

About this visualisation

Data sources

  • Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS), National Health Survey, 2022 — smoking, diet, alcohol, overweight/obesity and physical activity (national, by state & by sex).
  • Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW), Physical activity data tables, 2022 — activity by socioeconomic area.
  • Australian Sports Commission, AusPlay, 2025 — sport participation by activity and by state (the sport map, top-activities and frequency charts).
  • Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS), National, state and territory population (31 Mar 2026) — supporting population figures.

Notes

  • All health figures are percentages of adults for 2022 unless stated. "Exceeded the alcohol guideline" uses the 2020 Australian Adult Alcohol Guideline.
